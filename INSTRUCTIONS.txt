# Stream Multiview – How to use it

Watch several Kick, Twitch and YouTube streams and their chats at once.
In the app, this is in Settings > Instructions (gear button in the toolbar). F1 shows the keyboard shortcuts.
In a small window, toolbar buttons that don't fit move into the ⋯ (More) button at the right end of the toolbar.

## Getting started
- Download the installer from the project's GitHub page (Releases):
  - Windows: `Stream-Multiview-Setup-….exe`. Run it and follow the steps. It adds Stream Multiview to the Start menu and your desktop.
  - Mac: `Stream-Multiview-…-mac.dmg`. Open it and drag Stream Multiview into Applications.
- First time only: Windows may say "Windows protected your PC". Click **More info → Run anyway**. On a Mac, if it says the app can't be checked, open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**. This is because the app isn't code-signed yet.
- Running from the source files instead: install Node.js (LTS) and double-click **Start Stream Multiview.bat** (Mac/Linux: `start-mac-linux.sh`; on a Mac the first time, right-click it > Open With > Terminal). The first start installs the app engine (about a minute). **Create Desktop Shortcut.bat** adds a shortcut with the pear icon.
  - Keep that folder where it is after setup: the app runs from it.
- Coming from Kick Multiview: your logins, saved channels and layouts carry over by themselves. Running from source, copy the old folder's `node_modules` into the new one before the first start to skip the one-minute engine install.
- Your windows, volumes and saved channels are remembered between launches.

## Adding streams
- Paste a link or name into the box at the top and press Enter.
  - Kick: `kick.com/name` or just `name`
  - Twitch: `twitch.tv/name` or `tw:name`
  - YouTube channel: `youtube.com/@name`, `@name`, `yt:@name` or a `/channel/UC…` link (opens its live stream)
  - YouTube video: any video link (plays without a chat)
- The dropdown next to the box picks Stream + chat, Stream only or Chat only.
- If a YouTube channel isn't live, its window says so, checks again every 2 minutes and has a **Check now** button.
- Window dots show the site: Kick green, Twitch purple, YouTube red.

## Moving windows around
- Drag a window by its title bar. Resize it from any edge or corner.
- Each window's name is a dropdown: pick another channel to swap the two windows.
- Title bar buttons: volume and mute, + Chat, make big, save (☆), refresh, open in its own window, maximize, close. When a window is too narrow for them all, the rest are in its ⋯ menu.

## Layouts (Layout menu)
- **Focus view**: one stream (or another app's window) big with its chat, the rest in columns beside it.
- **Game focus**: your game big, streams, chats and other windows stacked on the side (see "Playing a game" below).
- **Columns**: every stream above its own chat. When there are more than fit, scroll sideways with ‹ › in the toolbar, the scrollbar at the bottom, or the mouse wheel over any title bar.
  - A strip above the columns has ‹ › to step through the streams (they loop around at either end) and a list to jump to any stream. "Step: 1 / Step: page" sets whether the arrows move one stream or a whole page.
  - **Highlight** (corner-arrows button on a stream): that stream goes big on the left with its chat beside it, and stays put while you scroll the other columns. Click it again to put it back.
  - In the highlight view, the **Focus area** under the big stream has a button for every open stream: click a name to make it big, drag one onto another to swap them (and their columns), × closes a stream and its chat (Undo brings them back). Each button has pause / play (play jumps back to live), mute and volume, and its arrow opens a small always-muted live preview. A preview is a second copy of the stream, so it uses some extra bandwidth.
  - "Side chats" (top of the Focus area) sets how many stream + chat columns sit beside the big stream: Auto or 1–5. The name at the top of each side chat is a dropdown to show any stream there.
  - The buttons at the top of the Focus area switch to **Columns view** (no highlight) or back to **Chat focus view**.
- **Chat focus**: streams down the side at exact video size, chats get the rest. Pick 1, 2 or 3 stream columns under Options in the Layout menu (or pick Chat focus again). Drag a stream onto another to swap them (their chats swap too).
- **Auto layout** and **Tile**: arrange everything to fit the screen. With "Fit in channels that open by themselves" on (Settings > Layouts), a saved channel that opens because it went live is added as the last stream and Auto layout is redone, keeping the other streams in order.
- The Layout menu shows the current layout's options under its pictures (point at one to read what it does). The ⚙ at its top right opens Settings > Layouts: every layout with its options, where the switch control shows, and your layouts.
- **Your layouts**: type a name and press Save to keep the current arrangement (windows, order, columns, highlight). Click it later to go back to it: windows already open stay open, missing ones reopen. In Settings > Layouts they're listed under the built-in layouts: rename, update (with the current arrangement) or delete them there. Each keeps its own copy of the options of the layout it was made from (Linked, stream columns, fitting in channels that open by themselves), so changing them doesn't change the built-in layout's.
- **Linked** (in Columns and Focus view, switched in the Layout menu's Options): a stream and its chat move together. Drag a title bar to reorder columns; the others slide aside to show where it will land, and dragging to the screen edge scrolls. Turn Linked off to move and resize windows freely.

## The switch control
- A list of every open stream with pause / play, mute, volume, refresh, a small live preview (the arrow) and remove (×).
- Click a name to make that stream big. Drag one name onto another to swap two streams.
- Shift-click a mute button to hear only that stream.
- Where it lives (toolbar Switch control button, or Layout menu > Switch control):
  - Docked: under the big stream in Focus view.
  - Floating: a panel you can drag and resize anywhere, in every layout.
  - Own window: its own window, even on another monitor. The pin keeps it on top.
- "Pause all except focus" pauses every stream except the big one.

## Playing a game (Game focus)
- Pick Layout > Game focus (or press G). The big area on the left is for your game, everything else goes in the side panel.
- **Put a game here** (Windows): start your game in **Windowed** mode, click **Put a game here** in the game area's bar and pick the game. Its window moves into the game area and stays there when you change the panel size or move Stream Multiview. Click it and play.
  - **No border** hides the game's own title bar so it fills the area. **Play** clicks you into the game. **Release** puts the game back where it was (leaving Game focus or closing the app does this too).
  - If a game won't resize, set it to Windowed mode in its own settings and try again.
- **See-through (play)**: the game area is a see-through hole. Whatever is behind Stream Multiview shows there and you can click into it. (On Linux, put the game behind the app yourself.)
  - "Keep on top" keeps your streams visible when you click into the game.
  - Any window you drag over the game area stays visible on top of the game.
  - Works on Windows (and Linux). On a Mac use "Show a window" instead.
- **Show a window**: put a stream or another app's window in the big area instead, using the dropdown or Pick window. Another app's window is only a picture, so you can't play a game through it.
- S / M / L sets the side panel width, and "Panel left / right" moves it to the other side.
- Pick any other layout to go back to normal.

## Settings (gear button)
- Click the gear in the toolbar, or press Ctrl+, (Mac: ⌘,). It's also in the menu bar: on a Mac in the Stream Multiview menu, on Windows and Linux in File.
- **General**: start new streams muted, snap windows while you drag, auto-pause hidden streams, and keyboard shortcuts on or off.
- **Accounts**: logins, Import follows, "Forget my login" and "Watch signed in".
- **Notifications**: messages in the app (on or off, and where they show: top, bottom or a corner), and desktop notifications: on or off for everything, live alerts for saved channels, and a test to check your computer lets them through.
- **YouTube**: your YouTube login, skipping ads, YouTube's cookie question and the YouTube API key (see below).
- **Mentions**: your Kick and Twitch usernames, other words that mean you (nicknames), and how a chat window gets your attention when someone mentions you (see Alerts below).
- **Spam alerts**: for moderators (see below).
- **Themes**: the app's colours.
- **Updates**: your version, checking for a new one, and what's new in each version.
- **Instructions** and **Keyboard shortcuts**: this help. The Help menu in the menu bar opens them too.

## Colours and themes
- Open Settings > Themes.
- Pick a ready-made theme: Pear, Purple Night, Ocean, Sunset, Cherry, Neon Arcade, Retro Amber, Midnight Mono, Daylight (light), Paper (light), Hikari (blue and white) or Tron (princess) (pink).
- Change any colour with its colour box, or type a colour code like #3fb6ff. Changes show right away.
- **Window border** is the outline around the selected and big windows. It matches Accent until you change it, so you can tone it down without changing the buttons.
- If a colour you pick would make buttons hard to read, the app keeps their text readable and says why under the colours.
- Type a name and click **Save theme** to keep it under "Your themes". The × deletes one of yours.
- **Back to Pear** returns to the original colours.

## Undo and redo
- The curved arrows in the toolbar undo and redo changes to your windows: opening, closing, moving, resizing, swapping and layouts.
- Keyboard: Ctrl+Z to undo, Ctrl+Y to redo (on a Mac: ⌘Z and ⌘⇧Z).

## Showing another app's window (e.g. Discord)
- Click the screen button next to Add (or press W) and pick a window, like a Discord pop-out call or stream.
- It appears as a live picture you can move and resize like any other window.
- To click or type in it, use the real window. It can sit behind Stream Multiview, but don't minimize it.
- The refresh button on that window lets you pick a different window.
- They show up in the switch control too: click the name to make it the big one, drag it onto another name to swap places, pause to freeze the picture.
- These windows aren't kept when you close the app.

## Saved channels (★ Saved)
- Click ☆ on any window to save its channel. Click a saved channel to open it.
- Tabs: All, Kick, Twitch, YouTube. K / T / Y badges show each channel's site.
- Live channels are listed first, with viewers and title. The Saved button shows how many are live.
- Bell: desktop notification when that channel goes live.
- Lightning: open that channel automatically when it goes live, once per broadcast (if you close it, it won't reopen until they next go live).
- Import: add the channels you follow on Kick, Twitch or YouTube (you need to be logged in to that site in the app). For Kick, each channel in the import list also has Unfollow.
- Open live, Open all and Save all open are at the bottom.
- Person button on a row: that streamer's profile (see below).

## Streamer profiles (Profiles button)
- Profiles lists your saved streamers. Search by name, nickname or tag, filter (Kick / Twitch / YouTube, Live now, Favourites, Has notes, Tagged) and sort.
- Type a name that isn't saved and click Look it up to find that name on Kick, Twitch and YouTube, then view or save it.
- Click a streamer for their profile. It also opens from the person button on a saved channel or on a stream's window.
- What the site says: picture, banner, about, followers, links, the live stream (title, game, viewers, when it started, chat rules) and subscriber badges.
- What the app records: each stream it sees while it's open (start, end, title, game, viewer counts once a minute) and a follower count each day. From that: live history, the "when they usually stream" chart, usual start time, average length, streak, average and peak viewers, top games and a viewer graph of the current or last stream.
- Kick and Twitch past broadcasts fill in streams the app didn't see (marked VOD), when the streamer keeps them. Each number says where it comes from, and the line under the numbers says since when the app has been tracking and how many streams it saw itself.
- Your notes, tags, a nickname and a favourite star are kept on this computer.
- Unfollow on Kick unfollows on kick.com itself (it asks first, and can also remove them from Saved). You need to be logged in to Kick in the app.
- The sites are only asked when you open a profile or look a name up; the history comes from the live checks the app already does.

## Logging in (person button)
- Log in once to Kick and Twitch (for Kick, enter the email code once). Every chat for that site uses the login, and it's kept when you restart the app.
- The person button logs you in and out, and has Switch and Import follows. Settings > Accounts has the same, plus the options below. The menu bar's Account menu logs you in or out of each site too.
- YouTube sign-in currently only works through the Firefox browser (Google blocks signing in inside apps, and Chrome and Edge lock their logins). Sign in to youtube.com in Firefox, then click YouTube's Log in (or "Sign in to YouTube with Firefox") and **Copy my login from Firefox**. Only the YouTube/Google login is copied, straight from Firefox: no extension, no files.
- You don't need to sign in to watch YouTube. Signing in is only for typing in YouTube chat, importing follows and "Watch signed in".
- "Forget my login when I close the app" (Settings > Accounts): tick a site and its login is wiped every time you close the app.
- "Watch signed in" (Settings > Accounts): tick a site and its streams play with your login too, so you watch as your account instead of as a logged-out viewer. It's off at first and uses more memory. Open streams of that site restart when you change it. Without it, Twitch Turbo and YouTube Premium don't remove ads from the video.
- The green lock means saved logins are encrypted on this computer and locked to your user account.
- Mac: the first time a new version opens, macOS may ask if Stream Multiview can use "Stream Multiview Safe Storage" in your Keychain. Type your Mac password and click **Always Allow**. That's the key your saved logins are encrypted with; if you click Deny, the app can't read them and you'll have to log in again.

## Alerts
- Mentions: when someone writes @you or replies to you in a Kick or Twitch chat, that chat window lights up and it's listed under the @ button. Set your usernames in Settings > Mentions, or click Detect.
- Also alert me for (Settings > Mentions): add nicknames or other words that mean you. A message with one counts as a mention, with or without an @. Whole words only, any case: "pear" finds "hi Pear!" but not "pears". Your own messages never count. They're listed as "Word".
- The number on the @ button counts mentions you haven't seen. Clicking into that chat clears that chat's; the @ list shows the latest five and clears those.
- All mentions (at the bottom of the @ list) opens the history: the last 500 mentions, replies and words, grouped by day. Search by name, message or chat, show only Mentions, Replies or Words, one chat, or unread ones. Click one to go to that chat; hover one to delete it. Mark all read, Clear all. Closing it counts them all as seen.
- To stop mentions from one chat, click the @ button on that chat window's bar (click it again to turn them back on).
- Settings > Mentions sets how: the window's border glows briefly or stays lit until you click that chat, and can get stronger while you haven't seen it (pulse, then flash). Pick its colour, a sound (or your own sound file), whether the sound repeats, and when to show a desktop notification.
- Live alerts come from your saved channels while the app is running.

## Spam alerts (for moderators)
- Settings > Spam alerts. Off until you turn it on.
- It only checks Kick and Twitch chats where you're a moderator (the ones with the mod view button). When it spots a promo-bot message, it tags it "LIKELY SPAM" in the chat and lights up that chat window, so you can deal with it. It never hides, deletes or reports anything.
- A message is only flagged when several signs add up, so normal links and clip links aren't flagged. The signs: a link written so the chat doesn't make it a link (`site com`, `site[.]com`, `site dot com`), promo words like "upgrades" or "zero cost", emotes breaking up the words, the same message in another open chat, and someone who just started chatting. Hover the tag to see which signs it found.
- Lists: sites that never count (Kick, Twitch, YouTube, Discord and so on) and the promo words. Add your own or reset them.
- Sensitivity: Strict flags more, Relaxed fewer.
- Try it: paste a message to see whether it would be flagged, and why.
- History: the last 50 flagged messages, with their score and the signs found. Click one to show that chat.
- The alert options are the same as for mentions, with their own colour and sound.

## Chrome extensions (Settings > Extensions)
- Install one three ways: **Browse Chrome Web Store** (find it and click **Add to Stream Multiview**), **Install from link** (paste its Web Store link), or **Load folder** (an unpacked extension). The app asks first, and shows what it can do.
- Pick where it's on: Kick, Twitch and YouTube, for streams and for chats. New ones are on for streams only. If you watch a site signed in, its chats and streams share one switch. Sites an extension doesn't run on are marked.
- Extensions in a stream or chat window's ⋯ menu lists the extensions in it. Click one to open its popup, the gear for its options. On a stream you can switch one on or off for that stream only.
- Each chat site and each stream keeps its own extension settings.
- Some extensions need Chrome features the app doesn't have; they're marked. Blockers like uBlock Origin Lite won't block anything.
- Extensions can read and change the pages they're on, including while you're signed in. Only install ones you trust.
- Extensions from the Web Store update by themselves.

## YouTube ads and the cookie question (Settings > YouTube)
- **Skip ads** (on to start with): when an ad shows its Skip button, the app presses it for you. Ads without a Skip button still play.
- **Answer the cookie question** (on to start with, "Reject all"): in some countries YouTube asks about cookies before a stream plays, and keeps it paused until you answer. The app answers for you with the choice you pick. Each stream remembers the answer, so it's asked once per channel. Turn it off to answer it yourself in the player.
- If YouTube changes the question so the app can't find the button, the app answers "Reject all" by itself and starts the stream again.

## YouTube API key (optional)
Live alerts check your saved channels while the app is running. Kick (when you're logged in) and Twitch answer for many channels at once, so they're checked every 30 seconds. YouTube is checked one channel at a time by reading its page, which is slower and can break when YouTube changes its pages. A free YouTube API key from Google makes YouTube's checks faster and more reliable. You don't need one: without it, everything still works.
- Getting one (about 5 minutes, needs a Google account):
  - Go to console.cloud.google.com and create a project (any name, e.g. "Stream Multiview").
  - In "APIs & Services" > "Library", find **YouTube Data API v3** and click **Enable**.
  - In "APIs & Services" > "Credentials", click **Create credentials** > **API key** and copy it.
  - Recommended: click the key, and under "API restrictions" choose **Restrict key** and tick only YouTube Data API v3, so the key can't be used for anything else.
- Google's own guide, with pictures: developers.google.com/youtube/v3/getting-started
- Add it in Settings > YouTube, under YouTube API key: paste the key and click **Add**. The app tests it once with Google.
- It's free: Google gives each key 10,000 "units" a day. With the key, YouTube is checked every 30 seconds too, using 1 unit per 50 videos it asks about (about 3,000 a day for 30 channels), plus 1 the first time for each @handle channel.
- The key is stored encrypted on this computer (like your logins) and only used to ask YouTube which videos are live. Remove it from the same place.
- If the key runs out of today's units, Google is busy, or the key stops working, the app goes back to reading pages by itself and Settings says why. Out of units: it tries the key again when Google resets them (midnight Pacific time). Too many requests: after 15 minutes, then longer.

## Saving bandwidth
- Auto-pause hidden streams (Settings > General): streams you can't see pause after 2 seconds and jump back to live when you can.
- Pop out streams: move every stream into a separate window (it opens on your second monitor if you have one). Chats stay here, and new channels go to the right window by themselves. In the streams window, **Tile** gives every stream equal space and **Spotlight** makes the stream you last clicked big; drag one stream onto another to swap them. "Bring streams back" or closing that window returns them.
- Pop out hidden (switch control): send only the streams you can't see to that window.

## Other handy things
- Chat − 100% +: make the text in every chat smaller or bigger.
- Hide bar: hides the toolbar. Move the mouse to the top edge or press Esc to bring it back.
- F11: full screen.
- Clear: closes every window (Ctrl+Z brings them back).

## Keyboard shortcuts
To switch every shortcut off, use Settings > General (or "Use keyboard shortcuts" in Settings > Keyboard shortcuts).
Shortcuts work when the app has your keyboard. If you just clicked inside a stream or chat, click a title bar or an empty spot first.

### General
- F1 or ? : the keyboard shortcuts
- Ctrl+, (Mac: ⌘,) : Settings
- Ctrl+Z (Mac: ⌘Z) : undo
- Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z (Mac: ⌘⇧Z) : redo
- N or / : type a link or name
- B : saved channels
- W : show another app's window
- H : hide or show the toolbar
- Esc : close menus and dialogs
- F11 : full screen

### Layouts
- F : Focus view (again for Columns)
- G : Game focus
- C : Columns
- Shift+C : Chat focus
- T : Tile
- A : Auto layout
- L : Layout menu
- S : switch control on or off

### The big stream (in Focus view; otherwise the first stream)
- 1 to 9 : make that stream big
- Left / Right arrows : previous or next stream big (in Columns: scroll)
- M : mute or unmute
- Shift+M : mute every stream
- Space or P : pause or play
- Up / Down arrows : volume
- R : refresh
- Shift+R : refresh every stream

## Updates
- The app checks the project's GitHub page for new versions a few seconds after it starts, then every few hours.
- When there's a new version it downloads it by itself, then shows a box in the corner. Installed with the .exe: click **Restart and install** and it updates and reopens. Mac: click **Install**, then drag Stream Multiview into Applications to replace the old one.
- Settings > Updates shows your version and what's new, lets you check right away, and has the "Update automatically" switch. "Check for updates" in the Help menu (on a Mac, also in the Stream Multiview menu) opens it and checks.
- Your logins, saved channels, layouts and themes are kept. The old app files are backed up first.
- If an update changes the app engine, close the app and start it again with your shortcut. It reinstalls the engine once (about a minute).

## Keeping your logins safe
- Your passwords are never saved, only the "stay signed in" login from each site, and it is encrypted.
- Don't share the app's data folder (AppData\Roaming\Stream Multiview or Kick Multiview). Sharing the app folder itself is fine.
- If you think a login was stolen, change that site's password and use its "log out of all sessions" option.
- Don't run old copies of Kick Multiview any more: they share the same data folder but can't read encrypted logins (you'd just have to log in again).
